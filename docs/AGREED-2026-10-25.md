## Terms & Conditions — one new version, English and Spanish

1. **Section 1 rewritten.** The Operators act as brokers and intermediaries,
   and may act for both sides of one transaction. They read what members send
   *in order to protect anonymity and decide what is passed on* — explicitly
   not a check of any member, offer, document or price (keeps section 4 true).
   The platform is free. Physical commodities only. Not an exchange, clearing
   house, payment service, or broker-dealer in securities or other financial
   instruments. Operators never a party to a contract of sale.
2. **Section 8, commission.** No fixed % in the Terms — some commodities pay
   a fixed sum per unit. Instead: the basis (percentage, or a fixed sum per
   unit in the deal's currency) and who pays it are agreed **in writing with
   each party before the introduction**. Members acknowledge the dual role.
   Grounded in *Hurstanger v Wilson* [2007] EWCA Civ 299 (disclosing that
   commission "may" be paid is not informed consent) and *Johnson v FirstRand*
   [2025] UKSC 33 (no profit from the position without fully informed consent).
3. **Section 8, after introduction.** The Operators remain part of the
   communication until the deal closes or ends. **Exception:** a principal may
   share confidential documents directly with the counterparty only. That is
   not circumvention and does not affect the commission. Needed because the
   current section 8 ("deal directly… without the Operators' involvement")
   would otherwise make a principal's private document a breach.
4. **Section 15 replaced** — English law (of England and Wales) and the
   exclusive jurisdiction of the Courts of the Dubai International Financial
   Centre, citing Article 14(B) of Dubai Law No. (2) of 2025 by name, plus
   written consent to service by email and at the postal address given. Built
   on the DIFC Courts' own model clause (Practice Direction No. 2 of 2012).
   The email consent is there because of *Gulf Petrochem v PetroChina* (DIFC
   CFI 048/2023): email service failed where no written consent existed.
5. **New:** acceptance records and the audit trail are kept **even after
   membership ends or an account is deleted**.
6. **New:** obligations **follow a freelancer into any company** they later
   form or trade through. Many brokers sign as individuals and incorporate
   only once a deal closes.
7. **New:** the answers on a listing are **statements made under the Terms**,
   so a false one breaches section 7.
8. **New, prohibited conduct:** false offers or demands, false statements,
   false/altered/forged documents — bank documents, bills of lading, assay
   reports, KYC named explicitly. Consequences: immediate suspension or
   permanent removal, recorded, commission already due not cancelled, may be
   reported to the relevant authorities.
9. **Privacy Notice:** add postal address to what is collected. When the
   scorecard is built, disclose it.

10. **New, DECIDED 2026-10-25: duties limited to the Terms.** *"The Operators
    are intermediaries, not advisers to either party. Their only duties are
    those set out in these Terms."* Fits *Johnson v FirstRand* [2025] UKSC 33,
    where no fiduciary duty arose because the arrangement was incompatible
    with single-minded loyalty - which a broker openly acting for both sides
    is. The openness (acknowledged dual role, commission agreed per deal) is
    the protection; this sentence says so plainly.

## Research findings that shape the above

- A non-English company may choose English law: Rome I art. 3(1), no
  connection required.
- DMCC and Meydan have no courts; with no clause, disputes default to the Dubai
  onshore courts, in Arabic. DIFC opt-in is open to anyone (DIFC Courts' own
  site; *Miucit v Mahab* [2022] DIFC SCT 467, both parties outside the DIFC).
- DIFC judgments are enforced across Dubai by the Dubai Courts' enforcement
  judge, who "may not review the merits" (Law No. 2 of 2025, art. 32).
  Enforcement abroad runs through the UAE's treaties (art. 33) — country by
  country, **not verified**, check on the day it is needed.
- DIFC Courts read the contract under English law; procedure and remedies
  follow DIFC rules (2026 law-firm article — **partly verified**).
- RDC 9.53: no permission needed to serve outside the DIFC; 9.54 local law
  governs the method; 9.44 a contract-specified method is valid; 9.45 the
  address may be anywhere if the contract makes it clear.
- UCTA 1977 s.27(1): the reasonableness test (s.3) does not apply where
  English law governs only by choice and would otherwise be foreign — very
  likely this platform's case. **Caveat, not verified:** the otherwise-
  applicable law may have limits of its own.
- The UAE joined the New York Convention in 2006 (Federal Decree No. 43).
  Arbitration travels further if foreign enforcement ever becomes the concern.
