Review of MyPlatform Terms & Conditions (Version 3.6) against English law principles
Overall assessment
The document is already quite protective for a free, invitation-only B2B facilitation platform in physical commodities. It repeatedly emphasises that the Operators are only intermediaries, do no verification, give no advice, and take no responsibility for Participants’ deals. The limitation of liability clause is broad, the non-circumvention/commission clause is strong, and the “you do your own due diligence” theme is repeated effectively.
However, the document itself correctly states that it is not yet complete. There are multiple critical placeholders, the liability clause can be tightened, and a few areas carry risk under English law principles even in a pure B2B professional context.
Detailed review by topic
1. Placeholders (sections 10 & 16 + header)
What it currently does: Many legal details left blank (company details, data controller address, hosting region, international data transfer safeguard, supervisory authority, etc.). Document openly says it is incomplete.
Potential issues: Incomplete terms create uncertainty and can weaken enforceability. Data protection notice is currently non-compliant.
Suggested improvement: Fill every placeholder with accurate information before any real Participants are onboarded. Remove the “PLACEHOLDER — THIS DOCUMENT IS NOT YET COMPLETE” banner only after lawyer sign-off.
Urgency: Critical – High
2. Section 11 – Limitation of liability
What it currently does: Very broad exclusion of almost all loss (including financial loss, lost profit, opportunity, fraud of Participants, etc.). Saves liability that cannot be excluded (fraud, death/personal injury by negligence).
Potential issues: Under the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (UCTA), exclusions of liability for negligence (other than death/personal injury) and certain other losses must be reasonable. A blanket “no liability for anything” clause can be struck down or cut down by a court if challenged, especially if the Operators’ own negligence is involved. Also, liability for the Operators’ own fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation cannot be excluded.
Suggested improvement: Add a clear financial cap (e.g. “liability limited to the total commission actually received by the Operators in the 12 months preceding the claim” or a fixed low sum). Explicitly exclude liability for the Operators’ own negligence except where UCTA prohibits it. Keep the savings for fraud and death/personal injury. Consider a short “reasonableness” statement if space allows.
Urgency: High
3. Section 8 – Non-circumvention & commission
What it currently does: Strong 24-month non-circumvention, commission payable even if deal done outside the Platform, Operators may act for both sides.
Potential issues: Generally enforceable in B2B English law if clearly brought to attention and reasonable. 24 months is on the longer side but common in brokerage. Risk if the clause is treated as a penalty or restraint of trade in extreme cases.
Suggested improvement: Keep it. Consider adding that the commission obligation survives termination of the account and that the Operators may seek injunctive relief. Confirm with lawyer that the dual-agency disclosure is sufficiently clear.
Urgency: Medium
4. Sections 4, 5, 6 – No verification, own due diligence, no advice
What it currently does: Strong and repeated disclaimers.
Potential issues: Good. These help show that Participants were not relying on the Operators.
Suggested improvement: Keep almost as is. Consider one short sentence confirming that Participants acknowledge they have had the opportunity to take independent legal/financial advice.
Urgency: Low
5. Section 1 – Nature of the Platform
What it currently does: Clear that Operators are intermediaries only, not parties to any sale contract, not an exchange, etc.
Potential issues: Helpful for reducing the chance of being treated as a principal or fiduciary.
Suggested improvement: Keep. Optionally strengthen by stating that no fiduciary duty is owed beyond the express terms.
Urgency: Low–Medium
6. Section 15 – Governing law & jurisdiction
What it currently does: English law + exclusive jurisdiction of DIFC Courts. Explicitly references Article 14(B) of Dubai Law No. (2) of 2025 and states the agreement is “specific, clear and express”. Service by email + postal address.
Potential issues: This is well drafted for the current DIFC regime. Dubai Law No. (2) of 2025 (effective March 2025) requires opt-in agreements to be specific, clear and express — the wording tracks this language. English law + DIFC Courts is a recognised and workable combination for international commercial contracts.
Suggested improvement: Keep the core wording. UK lawyer should confirm the exact current text of Article 14(B) and whether any minor wording tweak is advisable. Also confirm service of process is practical for Participants.
Urgency: Medium (but important to get right)
7. Section 12 – Disputes between Participants
What it currently does: Operators not involved and released from claims.
Potential issues: Reasonable and helpful.
Suggested improvement: Keep.
Urgency: Low
8. Section 9 – Confidentiality
What it currently does: Basic confidentiality obligation on Participants.
Potential issues: Quite short. Does not deal with return/destruction of information or exceptions (legal compulsion, etc.).
Suggested improvement: Consider expanding slightly (standard commercial confidentiality language) once the lawyer reviews.
Urgency: Medium
9. Section 10 – Privacy Notice
What it currently does: Attempts to be transparent but contains multiple placeholders and is incomplete.
Potential issues: Currently does not meet UK GDPR / Data Protection Act 2018 requirements for a complete privacy notice. International transfers and retention periods are incomplete.
Suggested improvement: Complete all placeholders with accurate information. Have the data protection wording reviewed by someone who understands UK GDPR.
Urgency: High
10. Section 3 & 7 – Eligibility, anonymity, false statements
What it currently does: Good protective language on professional capacity, false documents, etc.
Potential issues: Solid.
Suggested improvement: Keep.
Urgency: Low
11. Section 14 – Changes to Terms
What it currently does: Material changes require acceptance; continued use = acceptance for non-material.
Potential issues: Acceptable for B2B, but courts sometimes scrutinise unilateral variation clauses.
Suggested improvement: Consider requiring affirmative acceptance for any change that affects liability, commission or non-circumvention.
Urgency: Medium
12. Section 16 – Operators’ identity
What it currently does: Currently an individual trading under his own name until company incorporated. Many placeholders.
Potential issues: Operating as a sole trader increases personal liability exposure until the company is properly set up and the terms updated.
Suggested improvement: Incorporate the company as soon as possible, update the terms, and ensure the limitation of liability clearly covers both the individual (for the interim period) and the future company.
Urgency: High
Key English law sources checked against

* Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 (UCTA) – reasonableness test for exclusion/limitation clauses in B2B contracts, especially negligence.
* Common law rules on incorporation, construction and enforceability of exclusion clauses.
* Misrepresentation Act 1967 (liability for misrepresentation cannot always be freely excluded).
* Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR principles (for the Privacy Notice).
* Established principles on non-circumvention / brokerage commission clauses in commercial contracts.
* Dubai Law No. (2) of 2025 (DIFC Courts) – especially Article 14(B) on opt-in jurisdiction requiring a specific, clear and express written agreement.

Bottom line
The document is already stronger than many platform terms. The biggest immediate risks are:

1. The unfinished placeholders (especially company details and privacy).
2. The very broad limitation of liability clause (needs a financial cap and clearer treatment of negligence to improve enforceability under UCTA).
3. Operating as an individual rather than through a limited company.

Once those are fixed and a UK lawyer has reviewed and approved the final version, the terms should give a solid layer of protection for this type of free B2B facilitation platform.
