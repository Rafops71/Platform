# Ideas: how MyPlatform wins trust (conversation with the owner, 12 Nov 2026)

Status: IDEAS ONLY. Nothing decided, nothing built. Revisit when the owner asks.

## The problem, in the owner's words (summarised)
- A brand-new platform, run by people without a track record in the field. Strangers must trust it enough to put themselves on it.
- Other AIs all gave the same standard answers. "Ours is free" is the only difference they saw.
- We are anonymous, so we cannot show off big company names like metals-hub and others do.
- What people hate most is WASTING TIME. The platform should bring together people who are ready: price, terms, paperwork and financial instruments agreed, then get it done.
- Buyers' biggest worry: does the seller really have the stock? If the seller is a trader, has he secured the allocation he is selling on?

## Our fixed caveat
We do NOT review, verify or vouch for anyone or anything (liability). Any "verified" idea below is for a LATER stage only, and only if it can be done without the platform taking on that liability.

## The hooks we already have
1. Honesty. 2. Free to members. 3. Anonymity until a commission agreement is signed. 4. A real, named, accountable operator (the Terms use the owner's own name). Members are hidden; the operator is not.

## New ideas (12 Nov)
1. **Useful before it's a marketplace (OpenTable model).** Something valuable with zero other members, e.g. a private "fake-offer warning signs" guide or check that a member runs on offers received elsewhere. (Must stay a general guide, not a verdict, because of the caveat.)
2. **Borrowed trust.** A known third party (lawyer, notary, inspection firm) does whatever checking is ever done, not the platform. Later stage.
3. **Show ticks, not names.** Anonymous listings with small ticks such as "has closed deals here before". Later stage, and only ticks the platform can state as plain fact without vouching.
4. **Small and deep first (Facebook-at-one-university model).** One commodity on one trade route the owner knows well. Ten serious players in one niche beat a thousand empty listings.
5. **The owner as first proof (Airbnb-founders model).** Run his own real broker deals through the platform: "the tool I use myself".
6. **Public zero-tolerance for time-wasters.** One proven fake or no-show and the account is closed; members in contact are told. Sells the "no time wasted" promise.
7. **Something buyers can't get elsewhere.** A short anonymous monthly note on what is sought and offered on the platform. Members only.
8. **Members hidden, operator visible.** Lean on a real person and a real address behind the platform.
9. **Earlier, private ideas (also 12 Nov):** the Operator as the product ("every introduction is checked by a person": wording must respect the caveat); recommenders' standing rises or falls with the quality of who they bring; a private record of closed introductions per member; a capped, waiting-list membership (scarcity).

## The stock/allocation worry (to think about, not decided)
The platform cannot prove a seller's stock (caveat). It CAN set the order of events: the commission agreement first, then the introduction, then the parties exchange proof directly between themselves (Terms: documents pass only between the parties). An idea to discuss: a standard "readiness" statement each side declares before an introduction (e.g. seller declares the allocation is secured in his name; buyer declares the financial instrument is ready), recorded as the member's own statement, with the zero-tolerance rule if it proves false.

## Owner wording rule (12 Nov)
Never describe people on the platform as "serious" buyers/sellers (or similar labels): it belittles members, as if others were not serious. Readiness ticks: only for Principals, optional, soft wording; "allocation" has no legal meaning (Sale of Goods Act 1979 s.4: contracts may be oral; s.5: future goods), so wording must distinguish "agreed in writing" from "paperwork to follow".

## DECIDED by the owner (12 Nov) - seller readiness ticks (not built yet)
