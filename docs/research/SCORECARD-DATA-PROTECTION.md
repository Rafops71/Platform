## What members may ask for

- **UAE PDPL Art. 13** - on request, free of charge, the types of data
  processed, the purposes, **"decisions made based on automated processing,
  including profiling"**, and who it is shared with. Before processing
  starts, the purposes, the recipients and the cross-border safeguards must
  be given. *(unofficial translation)*
- **UAE PDPL Art. 18** - a right to object to decisions from automated
  processing, including profiling, and to have a human review them; not
  where the processing is part of a contract the member agreed to.
  *(unofficial translation)*
- **UK GDPR Art. 15** **[verified]** - access to the data itself, its purposes,
  recipients, how long it is kept, and **"where the personal data are not
  collected from the data subject, any available information as to their
  source"**. Outcome records are written by Operators, not the member, so the
  source is "the Operators".
- **UK GDPR Art. 14** **[verified]** - when data is not obtained from the
  member, the member must be told it is held, what it is for, and how long it
  is kept.
- **UK GDPR Art. 22** - replaced from 2025-26 by new Articles 22A-22D (Data
  (Use and Access) Act 2025, in force 5 February 2026 per legislation.gov.uk
  **[verified]**). Their content was **not read tonight**.
- **UK GDPR Art. 27** **[verified]** - a controller outside the UK that falls
  under Art. 3(2) must name a UK representative, unless the processing is
  occasional and unlikely to risk people's rights.

## The UAE executive regulations

## What this means for the scorecard

1. **Members can probably ask to see it.** Under the PDPL (if it applies) and
   the UK/EU GDPR (if they apply), a member may request the data held on them,
   including outcome records and any score built from them, and who wrote it.
   Build it so an Operator can produce one member's record on request.
2. **A human decides, always.** The plan already says the AI summary advises
   and never decides or suspends. That keeps decisions out of "automated
   processing" in all three laws - say so in the console, and keep it true.
3. **Facts, not accusations** (as agreed): counts of recorded outcomes, each
   showing it was recorded by an Operator, with dates.
4. **The Privacy Notice must say it.** Section 10 does not yet mention
   outcome records or a scorecard. Art. 14 (UK) and Art. 13(2) (UAE) require
   members to be told before or when such data is kept. That is a new Terms
   version - the owner's decision, so it is drafted, not published.
5. **Open, for the owner:** whether UK or EU members will be taken (which
   decides whether the GDPRs and a representative apply), and whether to obtain
   the official Arabic PDPL text (the portal refused it from here).

## Can a departing or expelled member have their records deleted? (2026-10-26)

They can ask; records needed to defend the Operators' rights may be kept.
- UAE PDPL Art. 15 (unofficial translation): the data subject "is not
  entitled to request" erasure "if the request affects the investigation
  procedures and claiming and defending rights".
- UK GDPR Art. 17(3)(e) **[verified]**: erasure does not apply where
  processing is necessary "for the establishment, exercise or defence of
  legal claims".
So deal files, outcomes, signatures and the reason for an expulsion may be
kept for as long as a claim could be brought; contact details and content no
longer needed should go (sql/018's deletion already does this). A fixed
retention period is still the owner's decision.
